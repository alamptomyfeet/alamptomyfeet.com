30 Days of Scripture for Anxiety

Day 1 of 30

The Lord Is Near

Rejoice in the Lord always; again I will say, rejoice. Let your reasonableness be known to everyone. The Lord is at hand; do not be anxious about anything, but in everything by prayer and supplication with thanksgiving let your requests be made known to God.

And the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding, will guard your hearts and your minds in Christ Jesus.

PHILIPPIANS 4:4–7 · ESV

Inside the words

Will guard your hearts. Paul reaches for a soldier's word here, the word for a garrison keeping watch over a city. Philippi was a Roman colony (Acts 16:12), a city that knew the sight of Roman soldiers. And Paul was writing under guard himself, in chains, most likely in Rome (1:13; 4:22), though some scholars suggest Ephesus. He didn't yet know whether he would live or die (1:20–24).

So catch what he is doing. A man watched by soldiers tells his anxious friends, who were facing opposition and strain of their own (1:28–30; 4:2–3), that they are being watched over too, by a far better guard. Not by changed circumstances, but by the peace of God, which surpasses all understanding. It stands at the door of the heart and mind, the very places anxiety tries to get in.

And notice how that peace comes. Paul doesn't say, "Try harder not to worry." Anxiety and prayer are aimed at the same unknowns; one carries them alone, the other carries them to God. That's why he can say in everything. And the reason it's safe to bring them comes first: The Lord is at hand, near in His presence and near in His coming. God's own peace stands guard over you in Christ Jesus.

For your heart today

Maybe anxiety is a constant hum for you right now, or maybe it comes in waves at 2 a.m. This passage doesn't shame you for it; it invites you in.

Paul's pattern is simple and gentle: bring your requests to God specifically, and bring them with thanksgiving. Thanksgiving isn't pretending everything is fine. It's remembering who you're talking to: the God who has been faithful before, who gave His own Son for you, and who is near right now. This peace surpasses all understanding, so you don't have to figure everything out to be kept safe in Christ.

Over the next 30 days, we'll walk through Scripture slowly, one passage at a time. Today, simply start where Paul starts: with prayer.

Sit with this

Anxiety often hides something underneath: a fear we haven't named, something we're gripping tightly, a quiet lie we've started to believe. Jesus told those who believed in Him, If you abide in my word, you are truly my disciples, and you will know the truth, and the truth will set you free (John 8:31–32). That freedom is found in Him and His word, not in figuring ourselves out. So bring what's tangled to Him, honestly, and let Him begin to unravel it there.

1.What have you been rehearsing to yourself about this worry but haven't yet said plainly to God? What would it sound like to bring all of it to Him?
2.Underneath the worry, what are you most afraid will happen? What does that fear assume about whether the Lord is really near?
3.Is there something you've been trying to guard yourself, by planning, controlling, or staying on high alert, that He is offering to guard for you?

A prayer to begin

Lord, You are near. Here is what I've been carrying, and here is the fear underneath it. Show me what's true, and guard my heart and mind in Christ.

Today’s practice

Read verse 6 aloud slowly, once in the morning and once before bed. Each time, add one specific thing you're thankful for.

From the Teachers

optional reading

In his 1990 sermon "Peace – Overcoming Anxiety," preached on Philippians 4:4–9, Tim Keller describes biblical peace as confidence and trust in God's wise control of your life, with anxiety as its opposite. He also notes that many Christians are thrown off simply because their peace comes under attack, when Scripture tells us to expect that. (Paraphrased.)
