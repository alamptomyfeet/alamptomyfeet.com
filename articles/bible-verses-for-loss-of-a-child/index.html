Bible Verses for the Loss of a Child

For the mom or dad whose arms feel empty, with room for every tear.

November 28, 2026 · 4 min read

“The LORD is near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit.”

Psalm 34:18 (ESV)

There are no words big enough for this. Whether your child was little or grown, something feels out of order. Parents are not supposed to bury their children.

This page will not try to explain why it happened. It offers a few passages of Scripture, read honestly, and a God who stays near while you weep. If you lost your baby during pregnancy, Bible verses for miscarriage was written for you.

The Bible has room for a parent's cry

King David's son Absalom had led a rebellion against him and died in the battle that followed. When the news came, David went up to the chamber over the gate and wept: O my son Absalom, my son, my son Absalom! Would I had died instead of you, O Absalom, my son, my son! (2 Samuel 18:33).

Scripture records that cry without correcting it. If you have said, "I wish it had been me," you are not the first parent to say it.

Rachel weeping for her children

Through Jeremiah, God gave a picture of a mother's grief: A voice is heard in Ramah, lamentation and bitter weeping. Rachel is weeping for her children; she refuses to be comforted for her children, because they are no more (Jeremiah 31:15). Rachel had died centuries before; Jeremiah pictures her weeping as her descendants were carried into exile, and Matthew later quotes these words when Herod killed the little boys of Bethlehem (Matthew 2:16-18).

The Lord answers her with a promise, not a rebuke: There is hope for your future (Jeremiah 31:17). For Rachel, that meant her children coming home from exile. It also shows that God meets tears with tenderness.

Jesus wept

At the tomb of His friend Lazarus, Jesus saw Mary weeping, and Jesus wept (John 11:35). He knew He would raise Lazarus, and He still wept with those He loved.

A God who keeps count of your tears

David wrote Psalm 56 when the Philistines seized him in Gath. He prayed, You have kept count of my tossings; put my tears in your bottle. Are they not in your book? (Psalm 56:8). No tear you cry is lost. David also wrote, The LORD is near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit (Psalm 34:18). There is more on that verse in Psalm 34:18 meaning.

The Shepherd who carries the lambs

Isaiah 40 comforts God's people after a long season of judgment and exile: He will tend his flock like a shepherd; he will gather the lambs in his arms; he will carry them in his bosom, and gently lead those that are with young (Isaiah 40:11).

This is a picture of God's care for His people, not a statement about any one child, but it shows His heart. You see the same heart in Jesus, who took them in his arms and blessed them, laying his hands on them when people brought children to Him (Mark 10:16).

What David said about his infant son

Years earlier, David's newborn son became sick. David fasted and lay on the ground, and on the seventh day the child died (2 Samuel 12:16-18). Afterward David said, I shall go to him, but he will not return to me (2 Samuel 12:23).

Christians read those words in more than one way: some hear confidence that David would see his son again, others hear that he too would one day go to the grave. The verse does not spell it out, so it is better not to claim more than it says.

That child's death was announced by the prophet Nathan at a unique moment in David's story (2 Samuel 12:14). It is not a pattern for why any other child dies. When the disciples asked whether a man's blindness came from his own sin or his parents', Jesus said it was neither (John 9:1-3). If you are carrying guilt you cannot name, bring it to God plainly.

Grieving with hope

Paul wrote that neither death nor life ... nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord (Romans 8:38-39). He also wrote to grieving believers in Thessalonica about their dead, that you may not grieve as others do who have no hope (1 Thessalonians 4:13). He did not forbid grief. He gave it hope, the same hope John saw at the end of the Bible: He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and death shall be no more (Revelation 21:4). The tears are real now. They will not be forever.

For today

Pick one verse, read it slowly, and say your child's name to God. If you are angry, tell Him that too; Is it okay to be angry at God? may help. For the valley you are walking through, see Psalm 23:4 meaning. If the holidays are coming with an empty chair, see Grief at the holidays. A pastor, a Christian counselor or a grief group for parents can help you carry this. If you ever feel unsafe or in crisis, please reach out to local help right away.

Questions readers often ask

What Bible verse comforts a parent who lost a child?

Psalm 34:18 is a gentle place to start: "The LORD is near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit." Psalm 56:8, Isaiah 40:11 and Revelation 21:4 are also deeply comforting.

Is it wrong to feel like I can't be comforted?

No. Jeremiah 31:15 says, "Rachel is weeping for her children; she refuses to be comforted for her children, because they are no more." God put that grief in His Word, and He answered it with a promise, not a rebuke.

Will I see my child again?

Scripture does not answer every question. David said of his infant son, "I shall go to him, but he will not return to me" (2 Samuel 12:23), and Christians read those words in more than one way. What the Bible says plainly is that God is just and rich in mercy, and that for those in Christ, death will one day be no more (Revelation 21:4).
