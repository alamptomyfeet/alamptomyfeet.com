What Does the Bible Say About Lust? A Plain Reading of Scripture

What Jesus, Paul and the Psalms actually say about desire, temptation and a clean heart.

November 10, 2026 · 4 min read

“Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me.”

Psalm 51:10 (ESV)

If you searched this question, you are probably not looking for a lecture. Maybe you want to know where the line is. Maybe you are tired of a struggle you have not told anyone about. Either way, the best place to start is what the text actually says.

The Bible treats lust as a matter of the heart, not only the body. It is honest that this is sin, and it is just as honest that God forgives, cleanses and gives real help to change.

Desire itself is not the problem

Scripture does not treat sexual desire as shameful. God made people male and female, and the Bible gives a whole book, the Song of Solomon, to love between a husband and wife. Lust in the Bible is something narrower: desire that reaches for what is not ours to take, treating a person as something to use rather than someone to honor.

Jesus moved the question to the heart

In the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus took commands His listeners already knew and showed how deep they really go. After the command against murder, He spoke about anger. Then He turned to adultery: You have heard that it was said, ‘You shall not commit adultery.’ But I say to you that everyone who looks at a woman with lustful intent has already committed adultery with her in his heart (Matthew 5:27–28).

Notice the words lustful intent. Jesus is not describing a passing noticing or a thought that flashes by uninvited. He is describing a look that wants, and keeps wanting. His point to a crowd that thought they were safe because they had never acted on it was that God sees the heart.

Then He used strong language: if your right eye causes you to sin, tear it out and throw it away (Matthew 5:29). Jesus was not commanding self-harm. Even a removed eye would not change a heart. He was using vivid speech to say, take this seriously and cut off what feeds it.

Where lust comes from and where it leads

James, the brother of Jesus, wrote to scattered believers about how temptation works: But each person is tempted when he is lured and enticed by his own desire. Then desire when it has conceived gives birth to sin, and sin when it is fully grown brings forth death (James 1:14–15). He is careful to say God tempts no one (James 1:13). Lust grows in stages, which also means it can be interrupted early.

John sums up what pulls us away from God as the desires of the flesh and the desires of the eyes and pride of life (1 John 2:16). These are not new struggles. They are old and human.

Why Paul says flee

Paul wrote to the church in Corinth, a city known for sexual immorality, where some believers argued that what they did with their bodies did not matter. He answered, Flee from sexual immorality (1 Corinthians 6:18), and then told them why: your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you, whom you have from God? You are not your own, for you were bought with a price (1 Corinthians 6:19–20).

Fleeing is not weakness. It is wisdom. And Paul gives a promise to anyone in the middle of temptation: God is faithful, and he will not let you be tempted beyond your ability, but with the temptation he will also provide the way of escape (1 Corinthians 10:13).

Grace for the one who has already fallen

The Bible's most honest prayer about sexual sin was written by a king. Psalm 51's heading says David wrote it after the prophet Nathan confronted him about Bathsheba. David did not hide or excuse it. He prayed, Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me (Psalm 51:10).

That is the heart of the gospel for this struggle. If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness (1 John 1:9). And Paul wrote to believers still learning to fight, There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus (Romans 8:1). Shame tells you to hide. God's word tells you to come to Him.

For today

Read Psalm 51 slowly, as your own prayer. Then take one honest step: tell a trusted pastor, counselor or accountability partner, since change usually happens in the light and with company. Questions women often ask

Is it a sin to be attracted to someone?

No. Noticing that someone is attractive is not what Jesus condemned. He spoke of looking "with lustful intent" (Matthew 5:28), a look that dwells on and wants what is not yours.

What Bible verse helps with lust?

Psalm 51:10 is a good prayer to start with: "Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me." 1 Corinthians 10:13 also promises that God "will also provide the way of escape."

Can God forgive lust?

Yes. "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9). In Christ there is "no condemnation" (Romans 8:1).
