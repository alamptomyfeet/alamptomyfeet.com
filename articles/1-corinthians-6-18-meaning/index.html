1 Corinthians 6:18 Meaning: What “Flee From Sexual Immorality” Means

Why Paul told the church in Corinth to run, what he meant about sinning against the body, and the grace he wrote it from.

November 19, 2026 · 5 min read

“Flee from sexual immorality. Every other sin a person commits is outside the body, but the sexually immoral person sins against his own body.”

1 Corinthians 6:18 (ESV)

Most commands in the Bible tell us to stand firm. This one tells us to run. That is worth understanding, because Paul's reason for it is not shame. It is a high view of what your body is and who it belongs to.

1 Corinthians 6:18 means that Christians should not linger near sexual temptation or try to manage it up close, but get away from it, because the body belongs to the Lord, is joined to Christ, and is a temple of the Holy Spirit. Paul writes it to people he has just reminded were washed and made new.

What does 1 Corinthians 6:18 say?

In the ESV, Paul writes, Flee from sexual immorality. Every other sin a person commits is outside the body, but the sexually immoral person sins against his own body (1 Corinthians 6:18).

The word Flee is a present-tense command in Greek, which points to a habit of running, not a single sprint. "Sexual immorality" translates the Greek word porneia, a broad word for sexual relationships outside of marriage between a husband and wife.

Why did Paul tell the Corinthians to flee sexual immorality?

Paul wrote this letter to the church he had planted in Corinth, a busy Greek port city. Sexual freedom was ordinary there, and visiting prostitutes was widely accepted. Some in the church had carried that view in with them.

They had a slogan, which Paul quotes back to them: “All things are lawful for me,” but not all things are helpful (1 Corinthians 6:12). They seem to have reasoned that the body is temporary, like food and the stomach, so what you do with it doesn't touch your spiritual life. Paul answers, The body is not meant for sexual immorality, but for the Lord, and the Lord for the body (1 Corinthians 6:13).

He then reminds them that the body matters to God. God raised Jesus bodily and will raise us too (1 Corinthians 6:14). And for a believer, Do you not know that your bodies are members of Christ? (1 Corinthians 6:15). He specifically addresses the men who thought visiting a prostitute had nothing to do with their faith: to join yourself to someone sexually is to become one with them (1 Corinthians 6:16), but he who is joined to the Lord becomes one spirit with him (1 Corinthians 6:17). Verse 18 is Paul's conclusion: so flee.

What does "flee" mean here?

To flee is to put distance between yourself and the thing that pulls at you. It is not a sign of weakness. It is wisdom about how temptation works.

The clearest picture in Scripture is Joseph in Genesis 39. When his master's wife pressed him day after day, he did not stay to debate. Instead, he left his garment in her hand and fled and got out of the house (Genesis 39:12). He lost his coat and later his freedom, but he kept faith with God.

Paul gives Timothy the same counsel, and pairs it with something to run toward: So flee youthful passions and pursue righteousness, faith, love, and peace, along with those who call on the Lord from a pure heart (2 Timothy 2:22). Fleeing is never only running away. It is running toward God and toward His people.

In daily life, fleeing usually looks ordinary: closing the app, leaving the room, not being alone in certain places or at certain hours, putting the phone in another room at night, and telling someone you trust what tends to pull at you.

What does "sins against his own body" mean?

This is the hardest phrase in the verse, and careful Bible teachers read it in more than one way. Paul is not saying sexual sin is the only sin that can't be forgiven, or that other sins don't matter. He has just listed many other sins in 1 Corinthians 6:9–10.

The surrounding verses explain his point. Sexual union joins two people as "one flesh" (1 Corinthians 6:16), so this sin involves the body in a unique way. It takes a body that is a member of Christ and joins it somewhere God did not intend. Many readers also think Every other sin a person commits is outside the body may echo another Corinthian excuse, which Paul answers in the second half of the verse. Either way, his conclusion is the same: your body is not a side issue to your faith.

Why does the body matter so much to God?

Paul finishes the chapter with the reason: Or do you not know that your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you, whom you have from God? You are not your own, for you were bought with a price. So glorify God in your body (1 Corinthians 6:19–20).

In Corinth, temples were everywhere. Paul tells believers that God's own Spirit lives in them, and that Jesus paid for them with His life. Purity, then, is not about earning God's favor. It is about honoring a body God already values, already bought, and already lives in.

Is there grace for someone who has not fled?

Yes, and Paul says so in this same chapter. After listing sins, including sexual ones, he writes, And such were some of you. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God (1 Corinthians 6:11).

He tells the Corinthians to flee because they are already washed, not so that they might be. And for anyone in Christ who keeps struggling, There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus (Romans 8:1). God also promises help in the moment: God is faithful, and he will not let you be tempted beyond your ability, but with the temptation he will also provide the way of escape, that you may be able to endure it (1 Corinthians 10:13). Often that way of escape is the door Paul is pointing to: leave.

How can I live out 1 Corinthians 6:18 today?

Read 1 Corinthians 6:9–20 in one sitting so you see the whole argument. Then name one place where you tend to linger instead of leave, and decide ahead of time what fleeing will look like there. Tell a pastor, a trusted accountability partner or a Christian counselor, because running is easier with someone beside you. If you ever feel unsafe or in crisis, please reach out to local help right away.

Questions readers often ask

What is the meaning of 1 Corinthians 6:18?

Paul tells Christians to get away from sexual temptation rather than linger near it, because the body belongs to the Lord: "Flee from sexual immorality" (1 Corinthians 6:18). He writes it to believers he has just reminded were washed and made new (1 Corinthians 6:11).

Who was Paul talking to in 1 Corinthians 6:18?

The church in Corinth, a Greek port city where sexual freedom and prostitution were widely accepted. Some believers argued the body didn't matter spiritually, and Paul answers that "The body is not meant for sexual immorality, but for the Lord, and the Lord for the body" (1 Corinthians 6:13).

What does it mean to sin against your own body?

Paul explains in the surrounding verses that sexual union makes two people one, so this sin joins a body that belongs to Christ where God did not intend (1 Corinthians 6:15–17). He is not saying it is the only serious sin or that it can't be forgiven.

Is my body a temple of the Holy Spirit?

Yes, for a believer. Paul writes, "your body is a temple of the Holy Spirit within you, whom you have from God? You are not your own, for you were bought with a price" (1 Corinthians 6:19–20).

What if I have already failed?

Paul wrote this to people who had failed and been forgiven: "you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ" (1 Corinthians 6:11). Confess it to God, take the next step of fleeing, and let someone you trust walk with you.
