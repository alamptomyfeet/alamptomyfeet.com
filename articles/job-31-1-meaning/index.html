Job 31:1 Meaning: “I Have Made a Covenant With My Eyes”

What Job meant by a covenant with his eyes, why he said it in the middle of his suffering, and what it can look like for us.

November 21, 2026 · 5 min read

“I have made a covenant with my eyes; how then could I gaze at a virgin?”

Job 31:1 (ESV)

Most of us decide what to do with our eyes in the moment, which is usually the worst time to decide. Job decided ahead of time. One sentence from the book of Job still speaks plainly to that everyday struggle.

Job 31:1 means that Job had made a firm, settled promise, a covenant, not to let his eyes linger with desire on a woman who was not his wife. He made the decision before the moment of temptation, because he knew his eyes lead his heart, and he knew God sees every step.

What does Job 31:1 say?

In the ESV, Job says, I have made a covenant with my eyes; how then could I gaze at a virgin? (Job 31:1).

A covenant in the Old Testament is a solemn, binding commitment. Job uses that weighty word for a promise about something as everyday as where he looks. The Hebrew verb behind "gaze" means to look closely or fix your attention on something. It is not a passing glance. A "virgin" here is a young unmarried woman. Job is saying: I settled this long ago, so how could I now stare at her with desire?

Who is speaking in Job 31:1, and why?

Job is introduced as a man who was blameless and upright, one who feared God and turned away from evil (Job 1:1). Then he loses his children, his wealth and his health. His friends insist that suffering like this must be punishment for hidden sin.

Chapters 29 to 31 are Job's final answer to them. In chapter 31 he goes through his life area by area, saying, in effect, "If I have done this, let judgment fall on me." He covers honesty, faithfulness in marriage, fairness to his servants, care for the poor, trust in money and more, and he signs it: Here is my signature! Let the Almighty answer me! (Job 31:35). Then the narrator adds, The words of Job are ended (Job 31:40).

So Job 31:1 is not a rule Job hands to others. It is his own testimony under oath, the first thing he names when he describes a life of integrity. That he starts with his eyes tells us how seriously he took the inner life.

Why would Job start with his eyes?

He explains in the next verses. Does not he see my ways and number all my steps? (Job 31:4). Job lives as someone watched by God, not to be frightened, but to be honest.

He also knows how sin travels. A few lines later he speaks of the time when my heart has gone after my eyes (Job 31:7), and then of a heart enticed toward a woman (Job 31:9). Eyes lead, the heart follows, and actions come after. Job guards the first step.

Jesus teaches the same thing about the heart. In the Sermon on the Mount He says, But I say to you that everyone who looks at a woman with lustful intent has already committed adultery with her in his heart (Matthew 5:28). Job's covenant is an Old Testament picture of what Jesus later makes plain: purity starts on the inside.

Does Job 31:1 mean every look or attraction is sin?

No. Job is not vowing never to notice anyone, and Scripture doesn't treat a passing glance or the simple fact of attraction as sin. His word is "gaze", a lingering, deliberate look that feeds desire. That is the same distinction Jesus makes when He speaks of looking "with lustful intent." For a fuller answer, see What Matthew 5:28 means.

The verse also isn't only for men. Job speaks as a married man about women, but the principle of guarding your eyes and heart applies to anyone who follows Christ.

How can I make a covenant with my eyes today?

Job's covenant teaches us to decide before the moment, not during it. Practically, that may mean choosing ahead of time what you will not scroll past slowly, what you will turn off, where your phone stays at night, and what you will do when a familiar pull comes.

The Bible gives us a prayer for this: Turn my eyes from looking at worthless things; and give me life in your ways (Psalm 119:37). It also tells us what to look at instead: whatever is true, whatever is honorable, whatever is just, whatever is pure, whatever is lovely, whatever is commendable, if there is any excellence, if there is anything worthy of praise, think about these things (Philippians 4:8). A covenant with your eyes is not only a list of things to avoid. It is a choice about what to fill them with.

It also helps to say it out loud to someone. A pastor, a trusted accountability partner or a Christian counselor can help you keep a promise you could not keep alone. If you ever feel unsafe or in crisis, please reach out to local help right away.

What if I have already broken that promise?

Job was a godly man, but he was not sinless, and by the end of the book he humbles himself before God: I had heard of you by the hearing of the ear, but now my eye sees you (Job 42:5). The eyes that he guarded end up seeing God more clearly.

If you have failed here, you are not outside God's reach. If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness (1 John 1:9). And Jesus understands the struggle from the inside: For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but one who in every respect has been tempted as we are, yet without sin (Hebrews 4:15). Confess it, receive His forgiveness, and make the covenant again today.

Questions women often ask

What does Job 31:1 mean?

Job is saying he made a firm promise not to look with desire at a virgin: "I have made a covenant with my eyes; how then could I gaze at a virgin?" (Job 31:1). He decided ahead of time because he knew his eyes lead his heart.

Who said "I have made a covenant with my eyes"?

Job, in his final defense to his friends (Job 29 to 31). In chapter 31 he lists the ways he has lived with integrity, and he begins with his eyes. It is his own vow, not a command he gives to others.

What does "gaze" mean in Job 31:1?

The Hebrew word means to look closely or fix your attention on something. Job is describing a lingering look that feeds desire, not every glance or passing thought.

Is there a Bible verse to pray for my eyes?

Yes. Psalm 119:37 prays, "Turn my eyes from looking at worthless things; and give me life in your ways." It asks God both to turn your eyes away and to give you life in His ways.

What if I have already failed?

God forgives those who come to Him: "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9). Confess it, receive His grace, renew your covenant, and let someone you trust walk with you.
