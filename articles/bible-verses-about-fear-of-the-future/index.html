Bible Verses About Fear of the Future

What Scripture says when tomorrow feels heavy, including the woman who laughs at the time to come.

October 17, 2026 · 4 min read

“Strength and dignity are her clothing, and she laughs at the time to come.”

Proverbs 31:25 (ESV)

Fear of the future can be loud, like a diagnosis or a layoff, or it can be quiet, a low hum about your children, your marriage, your money, or simply growing older. Either way, it tends to arrive in the form of a question with no answer yet.

Scripture doesn't respond by promising that the future will be easy. It responds by telling you who holds it. The verses most worth keeping close are Psalm 31:15, Isaiah 41:10, Matthew 6:34, Romans 8:38–39, and Lamentations 3:22–23, along with a verse often printed on mugs and journals that says more than we usually notice: Proverbs 31:25.

The woman who laughs at the time to come

Many people know this verse as "she laughs without fear of the future." The ESV renders it she laughs at the time to come. Either way, it is easy to read it as a picture of a carefree woman with nothing to worry about. The context tells a richer story.

Proverbs 31:10–31 is a carefully crafted poem, an acrostic in which each verse begins with the next letter of the Hebrew alphabet. It opens, An excellent wife who can find? The word for excellent is the Hebrew chayil, a word for strength and worth that is often used of valor, even of warriors. Boaz uses the same word when he tells Ruth that everyone knows she is a worthy woman (Ruth 3:11). This is not a portrait of fragility. It is a portrait of quiet, faithful strength.

And her laughter isn't naive. Earlier in the poem we read, She is not afraid of snow for her household, for all her household are clothed in scarlet (Proverbs 31:21). She has prepared. She has done the faithful work in front of her. But the poem saves its deepest reason for the end: a woman who fears the LORD is to be praised (Proverbs 31:30). Her freedom from fear of the future grows out of a deeper fear, a reverent trust in God.

It's worth gently adding that many readers see this poem as the closing portrait of wisdom lived out, not a checklist for every woman to measure herself against. If you've ever felt crushed by it, you can set that weight down. The invitation isn't to be her overnight. It is to trust the God she trusted. Her strength was never self-made, and yours doesn't have to be either.

Your times are in His hand

David, hunted and surrounded, prayed one of the shortest and steadiest lines in the Psalms: My times are in your hand (Psalm 31:15). Not his enemies' hands, not chance, not his own careful planning. God's hand.

James is honest that we don't get to see ahead: you do not know what tomorrow will bring (James 4:14). He doesn't tell us to stop planning. He tells us to plan humbly, saying, If the Lord wills, we will live and do this or that (James 4:15). That is a freeing posture. You can make your plans with an open hand, because the future was never yours to hold.

And Jesus, knowing how easily we borrow tomorrow's trouble, says simply, do not be anxious about tomorrow, for tomorrow will be anxious for itself (Matthew 6:34).

Nothing ahead can separate you

Paul's list of things that cannot separate us from God's love includes a phrase that speaks directly to this fear: nor things present nor things to come (Romans 8:38). Whatever is waiting in the years ahead, the unknown diagnosis, the hard conversation, the empty nest, the loss you dread, none of it can reach far enough to pull you out of the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord (Romans 8:39).

That love was proven at the cross and sealed at the empty tomb. Because Jesus has already walked through death and come out the other side, the final chapter of your future is not in doubt, even when the next one is.

Mercy for each morning

When the future feels too large, Isaiah 41:10 brings it back to presence: fear not, for I am with you. God doesn't hand you a forecast. He gives you Himself.

And Lamentations 3:22–23, written in the rubble of a destroyed city, reminds us that God's mercies are new every morning. You won't receive the strength for next year today. You'll receive it when next year comes. Today's mercy is for today. You only have to live this day with God, and He will meet you in the next one when it comes.

Taking the next step

If a specific fear about the future has been following you, try writing it down plainly. Then pray Psalm 31:15 over it, My times are in your hand, and do the one faithful thing in front of you today. That's what the woman of Proverbs 31 did. She prepared what she could, trusted God with what she couldn't, and found herself free to laugh.

Questions women often ask

What does the Bible say about fear of the future?

Scripture never promises an easy future, but it promises God's presence and care in it. Psalm 31:15, Isaiah 41:10, Matthew 6:34, and Romans 8:38–39 all point to the same truth: your times are in His hand, and nothing ahead can separate you from His love.

What does “she laughs at the time to come” mean in Proverbs 31:25?

It describes a woman whose strength and dignity let her face the future without dread. In context, her confidence comes from faithful preparation and, above all, from fearing the LORD (Proverbs 31:30), not from having an easy life.

Is it wrong to plan for the future if I'm trusting God?

No. The woman of Proverbs 31 plans carefully, and James 4:13–15 encourages planning with humility. Trusting God means holding your plans with an open hand, not refusing to make them.
