Bible Verses for Loneliness

Scripture for the quiet house, the full room where no one sees you, and the long evenings.

October 22, 2026 · 3 min read

“Turn to me and be gracious to me, for I am lonely and afflicted.”

Psalm 25:16 (ESV)

Loneliness is not only about being by yourself. You can feel it at a crowded table, in a marriage, at church, or scrolling past everyone else's full life at night. It is the ache of feeling unseen.

The Bible takes that ache seriously. From the very beginning, God said, It is not good that the man should be alone (Genesis 2:18). Loneliness is not a sign that something is wrong with your faith. It is a real human pain that Scripture names, and one God answers with His presence and His people.

God lets you say it

Turn to me and be gracious to me, for I am lonely and afflicted (Psalm 25:16). David does not dress it up. He simply tells God the truth.

Psalm 142 goes even further: Look to the right and see: there is none who takes notice of me; no refuge remains to me; no one cares for my soul (v. 4). That is about as lonely as words get. Yet the very next line is I cry to you, O LORD; I say, "You are my refuge, my portion in the land of the living." (v. 5). The prayer begins with loneliness, but it is addressed to Someone.

He will not leave you

Be strong and courageous. Do not fear or be in dread of them, for it is the LORD your God who goes with you. He will not leave you or forsake you (Deuteronomy 31:6). Moses spoke these words to Israel just before they faced a new land without him. The writer of Hebrews picks the promise up and hands it to every believer: for he has said, "I will never leave you nor forsake you." (Hebrews 13:5).

Jesus ended His earthly ministry with the same promise: And behold, I am with you always, to the end of the age (Matthew 28:20). And Psalm 139:7 asks a question with only one answer: Where shall I go from your Spirit? Or where shall I flee from your presence? Nowhere. Not even the loneliest room.

Jesus knows this feeling

On the night He was arrested, Jesus asked His closest friends to stay awake with Him, and they fell asleep. Hours later, they all left him and fled (Mark 14:50). Paul knew it too: At my first defense no one came to stand by me, but all deserted me... But the Lord stood by me and strengthened me (2 Timothy 4:16–17).

When people fail to show up, the Lord still stands by. That doesn't erase the hurt of being left. It means you are never left entirely.

God sets the lonely in families

God settles the solitary in a home (Psalm 68:6). God's answer to loneliness is not only His presence but His people. The church is meant to be a family where the solitary find a place.

That can feel risky if you've been hurt before. But it may look like one small step: one text to an old friend, one invitation accepted, one honest sentence to someone at church. Often God's comfort arrives through a person.

A simple practice for lonely evenings

Pick one of these verses and write it where you'll see it at night. When the quiet feels heavy, read it out loud and add one honest sentence to God. Then, if you can, reach out to one person, even briefly.

If loneliness has turned into deep sadness that won't lift, please talk to a pastor, counselor, or doctor too. Asking for help is not a lack of faith.

Questions women often ask

What does the Bible say about loneliness?

It names loneliness honestly (Psalm 25:16, Psalm 142:4), promises that God will never leave His people (Deuteronomy 31:6, Hebrews 13:5), and shows that God places the lonely in families (Psalm 68:6).

Is it a sin to feel lonely?

No. God Himself said it is not good for man to be alone (Genesis 2:18), and Jesus felt deserted by His friends. Loneliness is a pain to bring to God, not a failure to hide.

What is a good Bible verse for loneliness?

Psalm 25:16 is a ready-made prayer: "Turn to me and be gracious to me, for I am lonely and afflicted." Hebrews 13:5 is a promise to rest on afterward: "I will never leave you nor forsake you."
