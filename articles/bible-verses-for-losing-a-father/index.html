Bible Verses for Losing a Father

For the son or daughter learning to live without Dad.

October 31, 2026 · 4 min read

“As a father shows compassion to his children, so the LORD shows compassion to those who fear him.”

Psalm 103:13 (ESV)

Losing your father leaves a quiet you can hear. Maybe he was the steady one, the person who fixed things and knew what to do. Maybe he was hard to reach, and you are grieving the conversations you never got to have. Either way, something in you is now living without him.

Scripture does not hurry you through that. It gives you words for the tears, a Father who does not leave, and a hope that is stronger than the grave. Psalm 103:13 is a good place to begin: As a father shows compassion to his children, so the LORD shows compassion to those who fear him.

A Father who remembers you are dust

Psalm 103 is a psalm of David, a song that blesses God for His forgiveness, His healing and His steady love. In the middle of it, David reaches for the picture of a good father bending toward his child. Then he says why God is so gentle with us: For he knows our frame; he remembers that we are dust (Psalm 103:14).

That means God is not surprised that grief has worn you out. He knows how much you can carry, and He is tender toward you right where you are.

Even Joseph wept over his father

When Jacob died in Egypt, his son Joseph did not hold himself together. Then Joseph fell on his father's face and wept over him and kissed him (Genesis 50:1). Later, after carrying Jacob back to Canaan to be buried, the family stopped at the threshing floor of Atad, beyond the Jordan, and he made a mourning for his father seven days (Genesis 50:10).

Joseph was a man of deep faith who had seen God work through everything that happened to him. He still wept, and he still took time to mourn. Grief is not a lack of faith. Jesus Himself said, Blessed are those who mourn, for they shall be comforted (Matthew 5:4).

Giving thanks for what he gave you

Proverbs is written largely as a father's teaching to his children: Hear, O sons, a father's instruction (Proverbs 4:1). If your dad passed something good on to you, a way of working, a sense of humor, a habit of prayer, that is worth naming and thanking God for. Some of his words will keep coming back to you for years.

When the relationship was complicated

Not every father was safe or present, and that grief is real too. You may be mourning the dad he was and the dad he never became. David prayed, For my father and my mother have forsaken me, but the LORD will take me in (Psalm 27:10). Scholars read that line in more than one way, but the promise at the end is plain: whatever a human father could or could not give, the Lord does not turn you away.

Another psalm of David calls God Father of the fatherless and protector of widows (Psalm 68:5). You are not too old, and your story is not too tangled, for that to be true of you.

A Father who runs toward you

Jesus told a story to people who grumbled that He welcomed sinners. A son wastes everything and finally heads home, rehearsing his apology. But while he was still a long way off, his father saw him and felt compassion, and ran and embraced him and kissed him (Luke 15:20). That is how Jesus describes the heart of God the Father. On the hardest nights of grief, this is the Father you are praying to.

Not left as orphans

On the night before His death, Jesus told His disciples He was leaving, and they were afraid. He said, I will not leave you as orphans; I will come to you (John 14:18). He was promising them His presence through the Holy Spirit and His return. Those words still hold for everyone who belongs to Him.

The hope ahead

Paul wrote to believers in Thessalonica who were grieving their dead, that you may not grieve as others do who have no hope (1 Thessalonians 4:13). He did not tell them to stop grieving. He gave them a reason for hope: For since we believe that Jesus died and rose again, even so, through Jesus, God will bring with him those who have fallen asleep (1 Thessalonians 4:14).

If you don't know where your dad stood with God, that is a heavy, tender question. You can leave him with the Judge of all the earth, who is just and rich in mercy, and you don't have to settle every question tonight.

For those who are in Christ, the last page is already written: He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and death shall be no more (Revelation 21:4). Until then, The LORD is near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit (Psalm 34:18). There is more on that verse in Psalm 34:18 meaning.

For today

Read Psalm 103 slowly, maybe with something of his nearby, and tell God plainly what you miss. If you are also grieving your mom, Bible verses for losing a mother may help, and if the first holidays without him are coming, see Grief at the Holidays.

If the grief feels too heavy to carry alone, let a pastor or a Christian counselor help you carry it.

Questions women often ask

What is a comforting Bible verse for losing a father?

Psalm 103:13 is a gentle place to start: "As a father shows compassion to his children, so the LORD shows compassion to those who fear him." John 14:18, Psalm 34:18 and 1 Thessalonians 4:13-14 are also deeply comforting.

Is it a lack of faith to grieve my dad so hard?

No. Joseph, a man of great faith, "fell on his father's face and wept over him and kissed him" (Genesis 50:1), and Jesus said those who mourn will be comforted.

What if my relationship with my father was hard?

That grief is real too. Psalm 27:10 says, "For my father and my mother have forsaken me, but the LORD will take me in." Whatever your father could or could not give, God does not turn you away.
