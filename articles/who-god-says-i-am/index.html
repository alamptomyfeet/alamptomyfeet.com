Who God Says I Am

Bible verses about identity in Christ, for women tired of the labels.

October 17, 2026 · 4 min read

“For we are his workmanship, created in Christ Jesus for good works, which God prepared beforehand, that we should walk in them.”

Ephesians 2:10 (ESV)

Most of us carry labels we never asked for. The tired mom. The one who messed up. Too much, or not enough. Single, divorced, childless, behind. Some were spoken over us by others, and some we've quietly given ourselves.

The Bible answers the question of identity from God's side. For those who belong to Jesus, Scripture says you are His workmanship (Ephesians 2:10), chosen (Ephesians 1:4; 1 Peter 2:9), loved as His child (1 John 3:1), holy and beloved (Colossians 3:12), made new (2 Corinthians 5:17), and free from condemnation (Romans 8:1). These are not affirmations you have to work up the feeling to believe. They are what God has declared.

Identity is something given

Ephesians 2:10 is beautiful on its own, but it shines brightest in its setting. Paul begins the chapter bluntly: And you were dead in the trespasses and sins (Ephesians 2:1). Then come two of the most hopeful words in the Bible: But God. Paul goes on, being rich in mercy, because of the great love with which he loved us, God made us alive with Christ (Ephesians 2:4–5).

He makes it clear that this is not your own doing; it is the gift of God, not a result of works (Ephesians 2:8–9). Only after all of that does he say, For we are his workmanship.

Many popular messages tell women, "You are enough." Scripture says something gentler and far sturdier. On our own, we weren't enough, and we never had to be. God's grace is enough, and your identity rests on what He has done, not on how well you are doing.

His workmanship

The Greek word for workmanship is poiema, simply something made, a crafted work. You may have heard that it means "masterpiece" or "poem." Our English word poem does come from the same family, but Paul's word is plainer and, in a way, more wonderful. Its only other use in the New Testament describes creation itself, the things that have been made (Romans 1:20).

The same word for the mountains and the stars is used for you. You are as much God's handiwork as the sky. And Paul adds that you were created in Christ Jesus, which means a new creation. If anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation (2 Corinthians 5:17). The old labels belong to the old life.

Chosen and beloved

Peter writes to believers who felt like outsiders and tells them they are a chosen race, a people who belong to God as his own possession (1 Peter 2:9). For women who have felt overlooked or left out, that is a remarkable word. You are not on the edge of God's family, hoping to be noticed. You are His. Paul tells the Ephesians that God chose us in him before the foundation of the world (Ephesians 1:4). Before you ever did anything right or wrong, you were wanted.

John can hardly contain himself: See what kind of love the Father has given to us, that we should be called children of God; and so we are (1 John 3:1). That small phrase at the end, and so we are, feels like John anticipating our doubts. It isn't just a title. It is true.

Known and not condemned

David prayed, I praise you, for I am fearfully and wonderfully made (Psalm 139:14). God knows you completely, including the parts you hide, and in Christ He does not hold them against you: There is therefore now no condemnation for those who are in Christ Jesus (Romans 8:1).

God once said to His people Israel, I have called you by name, you are mine (Isaiah 43:1). Through Christ, that same belonging is extended to everyone who trusts Him. Your past is real, but it doesn't get the last word over who you are.

Living from who you are

Paul ties it together in Colossians 3:12, calling believers God's chosen ones, holy and beloved, and only then urging them to put on compassion, kindness, humility, meekness, and patience. Notice the order. Identity comes first; the new way of living follows. You don't become beloved by becoming kind. You grow kind because you are already beloved.

This matters in ordinary moments. When you walk into a room where you feel you don't measure up, when a mistake replays at night, when someone's words cut deeper than they should, your worth is not up for a vote. It was settled at the cross, where Jesus gave Himself for you, and at the empty tomb, where God declared that work finished.

If you aren't sure you belong to Christ, the invitation is open. Grace is a gift, received by faith, not earned. You can come to Him exactly as you are and ask Him to make you His.

To start, choose one of these verses, read the whole chapter around it, and pray it back to God. When an old label comes back, and it will, answer it with what He has said.

Questions women often ask

What does the Bible say about my identity as a woman?

For women who belong to Christ, Scripture says you are God's workmanship, chosen, holy and beloved, His child, and free from condemnation (Ephesians 2:10; Colossians 3:12; 1 John 3:1; Romans 8:1). Your identity rests on His grace, not your performance.

How do I find my identity in Christ?

It begins with receiving Christ by faith, because identity in Him is a gift (Ephesians 2:8–10). It grows as you read what God says about His people, in context, and let those truths answer the old labels.

What if I don't feel like who God says I am?

Feelings often lag behind truth. John wrote and so we are (1 John 3:1) because God's declaration stands whether or not you feel it today. Keep returning to His word, and let it shape your feelings over time.
