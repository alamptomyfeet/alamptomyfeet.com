Psalm 51:10 Meaning: “Create in Me a Clean Heart”

What David was asking for when he prayed for a clean heart, why he prayed it, and how it can become your prayer too.

November 17, 2026 · 5 min read

“Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me.”

Psalm 51:10 (ESV)

Many people know Psalm 51:10 as a song lyric before they ever read it in their Bible. It is worth reading in its setting, because the prayer means more once you know who first prayed it and why.

Psalm 51:10 is David's request for God to do what David could not do for himself: give him a new, clean heart and a steady spirit after serious sin. It is not a promise to try harder. It is a prayer for God's own work inside a person, and it comes from someone who had already been met with mercy.

What does Psalm 51:10 say?

In the ESV, David prays, Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me (Psalm 51:10). The very next line keeps going: Cast me not away from your presence, and take not your Holy Spirit from me (Psalm 51:11).

The two halves of verse 10 say the same thing in two ways, as Hebrew poetry often does. A clean heart and a right spirit both describe an inner life that is pure toward God and steady in following Him.

Who wrote Psalm 51, and why?

The psalm's heading tells us: A Psalm of David, when Nathan the prophet went to him, after he had gone in to Bathsheba. The story is in 2 Samuel 11–12. David, the king, took another man's wife and then arranged for her husband, Uriah, to be killed in battle to hide it.

God sent the prophet Nathan, who told David a story about a rich man who stole a poor man's only lamb. When David grew angry at the rich man, Nathan said, You are the man! (2 Samuel 12:7). David did not argue. He said, I have sinned against the LORD (2 Samuel 12:13), and Nathan answered, The LORD also has put away your sin; you shall not die (2 Samuel 12:13). Real consequences still followed in David's family, but God's forgiveness was spoken first.

Psalm 51 is the prayer of that moment. It opens, Have mercy on me, O God, according to your steadfast love (Psalm 51:1). David does not appeal to his record or his good intentions. He appeals to who God is.

What does "create in me a clean heart" mean?

Three words are worth slowing down for.

Create. The Hebrew word here is the same one used in Genesis 1:1, In the beginning, God created the heavens and the earth. In the Old Testament, that verb is only ever used with God as the one doing it. David is not asking for help cleaning himself up. He is asking God to make something new, the way only God can.

Clean. This is the language of washing and purity that runs through the whole psalm: Wash me thoroughly from my iniquity, and cleanse me from my sin! (Psalm 51:2) and Purge me with hyssop, and I shall be clean (Psalm 51:7). Hyssop was a plant used in Israel's cleansing rites. David is asking for the real thing those rites pointed to, a cleansing God does inside.

Heart. In Scripture the heart is not only feelings. It is the center of a person, where desires, choices and thoughts come from. David knew his sin had started there, which is why he asks for more than forgiveness of the act. As he says a few verses earlier, you delight in truth in the inward being (Psalm 51:6).

Jesus taught the same thing in the Sermon on the Mount, that sin begins in the heart and not only in what we do. What Matthew 5:28 means explains how He said it. Psalm 51:10 is the prayer that answers that diagnosis.

Is Psalm 51:10 a prayer for Christians today?

Yes, and the Bible itself carries this hope forward. Through the prophet Ezekiel, God promised His people in exile, And I will give you a new heart, and a new spirit I will put within you (Ezekiel 36:26). What David asked for, God promised to give.

In Christ that promise comes true. Paul writes, Therefore, if anyone is in Christ, he is a new creation (2 Corinthians 5:17). And for the ongoing struggle, John writes to believers, If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness (1 John 1:9).

So a Christian prays Psalm 51:10 not to earn God's acceptance but from inside it. It asks God to keep doing the renewing work He has begun.

What does Psalm 51:10 not mean?

It does not mean God is reluctant and has to be talked into mercy. The psalm begins with His steadfast love.

It does not mean you must feel clean before you can come back to Him. David prayed this with his failure still fresh: my sin is ever before me (Psalm 51:3).

And it is not a one-time magic phrase. David asks God to renew a right spirit, which suggests ongoing work. Change in a heart usually comes over time, with honesty and help.

What does God want from someone who prays Psalm 51:10?

Near the end, David writes, The sacrifices of God are a broken spirit; a broken and contrite heart, O God, you will not despise (Psalm 51:17). God was not looking for a show of religion. He was looking for honesty, and He promises not to turn that away.

That is good news for anyone carrying a struggle with lust or an unwanted habit. You do not need polished words. You can bring the truth to God, ask Him to make your heart new, and trust that He will not despise a heart that comes to Him like that.

How can I pray Psalm 51:10 today?

Read Psalm 51 slowly, all nineteen verses, and pray verse 10 in your own words. Then take one honest step: tell a pastor, a trusted accountability partner or a Christian counselor, since change usually grows best in the light and with company. If you ever feel unsafe or in crisis, please reach out to local help right away.

Questions women often ask

What is the meaning of Psalm 51:10?

It is David's prayer for God to make his heart new after his sin with Bathsheba: "Create in me a clean heart, O God, and renew a right spirit within me" (Psalm 51:10). He asks God to do an inner work that only God can do.

Who wrote "Create in me a clean heart"?

David wrote Psalm 51 after the prophet Nathan confronted him about Bathsheba and Uriah (2 Samuel 12). The psalm's heading names that setting.

Why does David say "create" and not "clean"?

The Hebrew verb for create is the same one used in Genesis 1:1 and is used only of God in the Old Testament. David is asking for something new that only God can make, not a self-improvement project.

Can I pray Psalm 51:10 for a struggle with lust?

Yes. It was first prayed after sexual sin, and it fits any struggle you bring honestly to God. He promises, "a broken and contrite heart, O God, you will not despise" (Psalm 51:17).

Does God forgive after a sin like David's?

Yes. When David confessed, Nathan told him, "The LORD also has put away your sin" (2 Samuel 12:13). For believers today, "If we confess our sins, he is faithful and just to forgive us our sins and to cleanse us from all unrighteousness" (1 John 1:9).
