Is Lust a Sin? What Jesus and Paul Actually Said

Where Scripture draws the line between temptation, attraction and sin, and what God offers on the other side of it.

November 11, 2026 · 5 min read

“For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but one who in every respect has been tempted as we are, yet without sin.”

Hebrews 4:15 (ESV)

Most people who ask whether lust is a sin are really asking something more personal. Is the thought that crossed my mind a sin? Is being attracted to someone wrong? Am I failing God every time I am tempted? Scripture gives honest, careful answers.

Yes, the Bible calls lust a sin. But it does not call temptation or attraction sin. Lust is desire you welcome and feed toward what is not yours, and God answers it with both a call to change and real forgiveness.

Is temptation a sin?

The clearest place to start is Jesus Himself. The letter to the Hebrews was written to believers under pressure, tempted to drift back from following Christ. To encourage them, the writer pointed to their high priest: For we do not have a high priest who is unable to sympathize with our weaknesses, but one who in every respect has been tempted as we are, yet without sin (Hebrews 4:15).

Jesus was tempted, and He never sinned. So being tempted cannot itself be sin. A thought that arrives uninvited, or noticing that someone is attractive, is not what Scripture condemns. James describes the moment it changes: Then desire when it has conceived gives birth to sin (James 1:15). Sin begins when desire is welcomed and allowed to grow.

What did Jesus say about lust?

In the Sermon on the Mount, Jesus spoke to people who thought they were safe because they had never acted on their desires. He said, But I say to you that everyone who looks at a woman with lustful intent has already committed adultery with her in his heart (Matthew 5:28).

The key words are lustful intent. Jesus is describing a look with a purpose, one that dwells on and wants what belongs to someone else. This was not a new standard He invented. The Ten Commandments already said, you shall not covet your neighbor's wife (Exodus 20:17). Jesus showed that God always cared about the heart, not only the act. The same is true whether the one looking is a man or a woman.

What did Paul say about lust?

Paul wrote to the young church in Thessalonica, many of them new believers from a pagan culture where sexual freedom was normal. He told them plainly what God wanted: For this is the will of God, your sanctification: that you abstain from sexual immorality; that each one of you know how to control his own body in holiness and honor, not in the passion of lust like the Gentiles who do not know God (1 Thessalonians 4:3–5). Lust, for Paul, belonged to the old life of people who did not yet know God.

To the church in Colossae, right after telling them to set their minds on things above, where Christ is (Colossians 3:1–2), he wrote, Put to death therefore what is earthly in you: sexual immorality, impurity, passion, evil desire, and covetousness, which is idolatry (Colossians 3:5). Notice the order. He first reminds them that their life is hidden with Christ, then he calls them to fight. The fight flows out of who they already are, and there are practical ways to fight lust.

Does God forgive lust?

Paul did not soften the list of sins in 1 Corinthians 6, which includes sexual sin. But he did not leave his readers there either. He wrote, And such were some of you. But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God (1 Corinthians 6:11). The same people who had lived that way were now called washed.

That is why the Hebrews passage does not stop at Jesus being tempted. The next verse says, Let us then with confidence draw near to the throne of grace, that we may receive mercy and find grace to help in time of need (Hebrews 4:16). The answer to lust is not hiding in shame. It is coming near to the One who understands temptation and still offers mercy.

What should I do if I struggle with lust?

If you have been carrying guilt over every passing thought, let Hebrews 4:15 set you free from a false burden. If you have been feeding a desire you know is wrong, name it honestly before God and receive His mercy. Then do not fight alone: a trusted pastor, an accountability partner or a Christian counselor is a wise and good step. Questions women often ask

Is it a sin to have a lustful thought?

A thought that comes uninvited is temptation, not sin. Jesus was "tempted as we are, yet without sin" (Hebrews 4:15). James says sin comes when desire "has conceived" (James 1:15), meaning it is welcomed and allowed to grow.

Is it a sin to find someone attractive?

No. Finding someone attractive, or being tempted, is not itself a sin. Jesus was "tempted as we are, yet without sin" (Hebrews 4:15), so a noticing or an uninvited thought is not what Scripture condemns. What Jesus named in Matthew 5:28 is looking "with lustful intent", a look that dwells on and wants what belongs to someone else.

Is lust a sin in the Bible?

Yes. Jesus said that looking "with lustful intent" is adultery in the heart (Matthew 5:28), and Paul told believers to live "not in the passion of lust like the Gentiles who do not know God" (1 Thessalonians 4:5).

What if I keep falling into lust?

You can still come to Him. Paul wrote to people who had lived in sexual sin, "But you were washed, you were sanctified, you were justified in the name of the Lord Jesus Christ and by the Spirit of our God" (1 Corinthians 6:11).
