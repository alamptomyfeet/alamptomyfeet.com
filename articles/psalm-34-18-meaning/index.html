Psalm 34:18 Meaning: The Lord Is Near to the Brokenhearted

What David meant, where he was when he wrote it, and why it is still true for you tonight.

October 22, 2026 · 4 min read

“The LORD is near to the brokenhearted and saves the crushed in spirit.”

Psalm 34:18 (ESV)

Some verses are easy to read and hard to believe. Psalm 34:18 is one of them. When your heart has been broken, by a death, a diagnosis, a marriage, a child, a door that closed, God can feel very far away. This verse says the opposite.

Psalm 34:18 means that God does not keep His distance from people in pain. He comes close to them, and He rescues those whose spirit has been crushed. It is not a promise that grief will be quick. It is a promise about where God is while it lasts.

Where David was when he wrote it

The heading of Psalm 34 tells us the setting: Of David, when he changed his behavior before Abimelech, so that he drove him out, and he went away. The story is in 1 Samuel 21. David was running for his life from King Saul. He fled to the Philistine city of Gath, was recognized by its king, Achish (called Abimelech in the psalm's heading, likely a royal title), and was so afraid that he pretended to be insane to escape.

So this is not a psalm written from a palace. It comes from a man who had lost his home, his position, and his safety, and who had just been humiliated. Out of that low place he writes: I sought the LORD, and he answered me and delivered me from all my fears (Psalm 34:4).

What "brokenhearted" and "crushed in spirit" mean

In Hebrew, brokenhearted is literally "the broken of heart." The phrase crushed in spirit uses a word for something pounded down, ground small. Together they describe more than sadness. They describe a person whose inner strength has given way.

Notice that God does not wait for these people to pull themselves together first. The verse names them exactly as they are, broken and crushed, and says He is near to them. Isaiah 57:15 says the same thing from God's own mouth. There He is the One who is high and lifted up, and He says, I dwell in the high and holy place, and also with him who is of a contrite and lowly spirit, to revive the spirit of the lowly.

Read it with the next verse

Psalm 34:18 is often quoted alone, but verse 19 keeps it honest: Many are the afflictions of the righteous, but the LORD delivers him out of them all. Scripture does not pretend that faithful people avoid pain. Many are the afflictions. The promise is not that you will never be broken, but that brokenness is not the end of your story.

Verse 17 adds, When the righteous cry for help, the LORD hears and delivers them out of all their troubles. So the pattern of the psalm is simple: cry out, He hears, He draws near, He delivers. The deliverance may not come on our timeline, and some of it waits for the day when He will wipe away every tear (Revelation 21:4). But His nearness is for now.

Fulfilled in Jesus

Psalm 34:20 says of the righteous one, He keeps all his bones; not one of them is broken. John 19:36 applies this to Jesus on the cross: Not one of his bones will be broken. The One who is near to the brokenhearted is not a distant comforter. He was a man of sorrows and acquainted with grief (Isaiah 53:3). He wept at His friend's tomb (John 11:35). When you bring Him your broken heart, you are bringing it to someone who knows.

How to hold this verse tonight

Say it as a prayer. "Lord, I am brokenhearted. You said You are near. Be near to me now."

Let it be honest. The psalms give you permission to tell God exactly how crushed you feel. Nearness is not reserved for people who feel strong.

Read the whole psalm. Psalm 34 moves from fear to taste and see that the LORD is good (v. 8). Let David's story walk you forward.

Questions women often ask

What is the main message of Psalm 34:18?

That God draws near to people in deep pain and saves those whose spirit has been crushed. He does not wait for us to be strong before He comes close.

What was happening when David wrote Psalm 34?

He was fleeing King Saul and had just escaped the Philistine king in Gath by pretending to be insane (1 Samuel 21:10–15). The psalm comes out of fear and humiliation, not comfort.

Does Psalm 34:18 mean God will take my pain away?

It promises His presence and His rescue, but verse 19 admits that the righteous face many afflictions. Some healing comes now, and all of it comes in the end (Revelation 21:4).
