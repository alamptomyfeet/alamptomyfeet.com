What to Do When God Is Silent

When you have prayed and prayed and heard nothing, the Bible has more to say than you might expect.

October 22, 2026 · 3 min read

“How long, O LORD? Will you forget me forever? How long will you hide your face from me?”

Psalm 13:1 (ESV)

You have prayed the same prayer for months. You have opened your Bible and felt nothing. You have asked for a sign, an answer, anything, and heaven seems quiet.

If that is where you are, you are in good company. The Bible does not hide God's silence. It gives us words for it, shows us faithful people who lived through it, and points us to the One who has already spoken.

The Bible gives you words for silence

How long, O LORD? Will you forget me forever? How long will you hide your face from me? (Psalm 13:1). David asks "how long" four times in two verses. This is not a polished prayer. It is a complaint, and God put it in Scripture.

Psalm 22 begins even lower: My God, my God, why have you forsaken me?... O my God, I cry by day, but you do not answer, and by night, but I find no rest (vv. 1–2). Jesus prayed the first line of this psalm from the cross (Matthew 27:46). The Son of God knows what it is to cry out and feel unanswered.

So the first thing to do when God is silent is to tell Him so. Silence from God is not a reason to stop talking to God.

Remember what He has already said

Psalm 13 turns in its last two verses: But I have trusted in your steadfast love; my heart shall rejoice in your salvation. I will sing to the LORD, because he has dealt bountifully with me (vv. 5–6). Nothing in David's situation has changed yet. What has changed is where he is looking: back at God's steadfast love.

God is never truly silent toward His people, because He has already spoken. Long ago, at many times and in many ways, God spoke to our fathers by the prophets, but in these last days he has spoken to us by his Son (Hebrews 1:1–2). When you can't hear an answer to your specific prayer, you can still open His word and hear Him say what is always true. Your word is a lamp to my feet and a light to my path (Psalm 119:105). A lamp doesn't light the whole road. It lights the next step.

Silence is not the same as absence

In John 11, Mary and Martha sent word to Jesus that their brother Lazarus was sick. John tells us plainly, Now Jesus loved Martha and her sister and Lazarus, and then, So, when he heard that Lazarus was ill, he stayed two days longer in the place where he was (vv. 5–6).

Loved, and so He waited. By the time He came, Lazarus had died. The sisters' grief was real, and Jesus wept with them. But His delay was not indifference. He was about to show them something far greater than they had asked for.

That doesn't mean every silence ends in a miracle like Bethany. It means His love doesn't stop when you can't hear Him.

When you don't know what to pray

Sometimes the silence is on our side too. We run out of words. Romans 8:26 is for that moment: For we do not know what to pray for as we ought, but the Spirit himself intercedes for us with groanings too deep for words. Even your sighs are being carried.

What to do while you wait

Keep praying honestly. Psalm 13 is your permission.

Keep reading. Choose one psalm a day. Let God's word speak when your circumstances don't.

Wait quietly, not passively. It is good that one should wait quietly for the salvation of the LORD (Lamentations 3:26).

Don't wait alone. Tell a trusted friend or pastor you are in a quiet season.

Questions women often ask

Why is God silent when I pray?

Scripture doesn't always give the reason, but it shows that faithful people like David experienced it (Psalm 13, Psalm 22), and that God's silence is not His absence. In John 11, Jesus delayed because He loved the family.

Is God's silence a punishment?

Not necessarily. The psalmists who felt it most were often walking faithfully. If there is sin to confess, God welcomes that honesty (1 John 1:9), but silence alone is not proof that He is angry with you.

What Bible verse helps when God feels silent?

Psalm 13 gives you words for the complaint, and Lamentations 3:25–26 gives you a way to wait: "The LORD is good to those who wait for him... It is good that one should wait quietly for the salvation of the LORD."
