Bible Verses for Grief and the Loss of a Loved One

Scripture for the weeks after a loss, read in context, with room for your tears.

November 7, 2026 · 4 min read

“He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds.”

Psalm 147:3 (ESV)

When someone you love dies, even familiar verses can feel far away. You may not have the energy for long chapters right now, and that is all right. A few true words, read slowly, are enough for today.

The Bible does not ask you to hide your grief. It shows you a Savior who wept, a God who keeps track of every tear, and a hope that death does not get the last word.

Jesus wept at a friend's grave

When Jesus arrived in Bethany, His friend Lazarus had already been in the tomb four days. Lazarus's sister Mary fell at His feet in tears, and the mourners with her were weeping too. John tells us Jesus was deeply moved in his spirit and greatly troubled (John 11:33). Then comes the shortest verse in the Bible: Jesus wept (John 11:35).

Here is what makes that so tender. Jesus knew He was about to raise Lazarus. He wept anyway. Your tears are not a failure of faith. The Son of God shed them too, standing beside people who were hurting. Centuries earlier, Isaiah had described God's coming Servant as a man of sorrows and acquainted with grief (Isaiah 53:3), words the New Testament applies to Jesus.

There is a time to mourn

Ecclesiastes lists the seasons of a human life, and grief is one of them: a time to weep, and a time to laugh; a time to mourn, and a time to dance (Ecclesiastes 3:4). Mourning has a place in God's world. You do not need to rush to the next season before this one is done.

That is also why grief is meant to be shared. Paul told the church in Rome to weep with those who weep (Romans 12:15). If people want to sit with you, let them.

God keeps your tears

David wrote Psalm 56, according to its heading, when the Philistines seized him in Gath. Alone and afraid, he prayed, You have kept count of my tossings; put my tears in your bottle. Are they not in your book? (Psalm 56:8).

The sleepless nights and the tears no one else sees are not lost. God counts them. Another psalm says of Him, He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds (Psalm 147:3). In context, that psalm praises the God who gathers His scattered people and also numbers the stars. The God who holds the stars also bends close to a broken heart.

The God of all comfort

Paul opens his second letter to Corinth with a blessing he had learned through his own suffering: Blessed be the God and Father of our Lord Jesus Christ, the Father of mercies and God of all comfort, who comforts us in all our affliction (2 Corinthians 1:3–4). He goes on to say this comfort is meant to be passed on. Someday the comfort you receive now may help someone else in their loss.

Nothing can separate you from His love

Loss can make everything feel unsteady. Paul ends Romans 8 with a list of everything that cannot cut a believer off from God, and death comes first: For I am sure that neither death nor life, nor angels nor rulers, nor things present nor things to come, nor powers, nor height nor depth, nor anything else in all creation, will be able to separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord (Romans 8:38–39).

Death does not get the last word

Back in Bethany, before He wept, Jesus told Martha, I am the resurrection and the life. Whoever believes in me, though he die, yet shall he live (John 11:25). Then He called Lazarus out of the tomb, and not long after, He walked out of His own.

That is why Paul could write to grieving believers that they need not grieve as others do who have no hope (1 Thessalonians 4:13), and why he could taunt death itself: O death, where is your victory? O death, where is your sting? (1 Corinthians 15:55). The Bible's last picture of the future is God with His people, where He will wipe away every tear from their eyes, and death shall be no more (Revelation 21:4).

For today

Pick one of these verses and read it slowly, out loud, as a prayer. If your heart feels crushed, start with Psalm 34:18 meaning or Psalm 23:4 meaning. If you are angry, Is it okay to be angry at God? may help.

For a specific loss, there are articles on losing a mother, losing a father and miscarriage, and if the holidays are coming, see Grief at the holidays.

If grief feels too heavy to carry alone, a pastor or a Christian counselor can help you carry it.

Questions women often ask

What is a good Bible verse for grief and loss?

Psalm 147:3 is a gentle place to start: "He heals the brokenhearted and binds up their wounds." John 11:35, Psalm 56:8, 2 Corinthians 1:3-4 and Revelation 21:4 are also deeply comforting.

Is it wrong for a Christian to grieve?

No. Jesus knew He would raise Lazarus, and He still wept at the tomb (John 11:35). Paul tells believers not to grieve "as others do who have no hope" (1 Thessalonians 4:13), which means grieving with hope, not without tears.

What does the Bible say happens to believers who die?

Jesus said, "I am the resurrection and the life. Whoever believes in me, though he die, yet shall he live" (John 11:25). Paul adds that nothing, not even death, "will be able to separate us from the love of God in Christ Jesus our Lord" (Romans 8:39).
