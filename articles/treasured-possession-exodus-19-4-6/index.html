Who Does God Say I Am? His Treasured Possession

Before a single command, God said: I carried you, and I brought you to Myself.

October 29, 2026 · 5 min read

“You yourselves have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I bore you on eagles' wings and brought you to myself.”

Exodus 19:4 (ESV)

"Who does God say I am?" is a question many of us carry quietly. We often try to answer it by what we do: how faithful we have been this week, how well we are holding things together, how much we have gotten right.

In Exodus 19, God answers that question for His people in a surprising order. Before they had kept a single command, before the Ten Commandments were even spoken, He told them what He had already done for them and who they were to Him. Rescue came first. Belonging came first. Obedience was the response, not the entry fee.

The setting: a mountain in the wilderness

Israel had just come out of slavery in Egypt. On the third new moon after the people of Israel had gone out of the land of Egypt, on that day they came into the wilderness of Sinai (Exodus 19:1). They had crossed the sea, grumbled about water and bread, and watched God provide anyway.

Now they were camped at the foot of Mount Sinai, and Moses went up to meet with God. What God says next is the introduction to everything that follows at Sinai.

"I bore you on eagles' wings"

The LORD tells Moses to say to the people: You yourselves have seen what I did to the Egyptians, and how I bore you on eagles' wings and brought you to myself (Exodus 19:4).

Notice who is doing all the work in that sentence. God judged Egypt. God carried Israel. God brought them, and not just to a place, but to myself. The goal of the exodus was not only freedom from Pharaoh. It was nearness to God.

Moses later sang the same picture: Like an eagle that stirs up its nest, that flutters over its young, spreading out its wings, catching them, bearing them on its pinions, the LORD alone guided him (Deuteronomy 32:11–12). A young bird does not carry itself. It is caught and held.

"My treasured possession"

Then comes the invitation: Now therefore, if you will indeed obey my voice and keep my covenant, you shall be my treasured possession among all peoples, for all the earth is mine; and you shall be to me a kingdom of priests and a holy nation (Exodus 19:5–6).

It is honest to notice the word "if." God is calling Israel into a covenant, and He expects His people to listen and keep it. But look at the "now therefore." The call to obey rests on the rescue that already happened. God did not say, "Obey, and then I will bring you out." He said, "I brought you out. Now therefore, walk with Me."

The same order opens the Ten Commandments in the next chapter: I am the LORD your God, who brought you out of the land of Egypt, out of the house of slavery (Exodus 20:2). Grace speaks first, then the commands.

And why did God choose them? Moses tells them plainly: It was not because you were more in number than any other people that the LORD set his love on you and chose you, for you were the fewest of all peoples, but it is because the LORD loves you (Deuteronomy 7:7–8). Not because they were impressive. Because He loved them.

What this means for you

Exodus 19 was spoken to Israel at Sinai, and we should read it that way first. But the New Testament takes up its very words for everyone who belongs to Christ. Peter writes to believers scattered across many nations: But you are a chosen race, a royal priesthood, a holy nation, a people for his own possession, that you may proclaim the excellencies of him who called you out of darkness into his marvelous light (1 Peter 2:9).

And he adds a line that answers our question directly: Once you were not a people, but now you are God's people; once you had not received mercy, but now you have received mercy (1 Peter 2:10).

So who does God say you are, if you are in Christ? Someone He rescued. Someone He carried. Someone He brought to Himself. Someone He calls His own possession, set apart to make His goodness known. Your obedience matters deeply to Him, but it grows out of that identity. It does not purchase it.

A note on the other eagles' wings

Many people know a different eagle verse, Isaiah 40:31: but they who wait for the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings like eagles; they shall run and not be weary; they shall walk and not faint.

It is a beautiful promise, but it speaks to a different moment. Isaiah was answering a weary people who felt forgotten: My way is hidden from the LORD (Isaiah 40:27). There, the picture is of God renewing the strength of those who wait on Him. In Exodus 19, the eagle is God Himself carrying His people out of slavery. Both belong together in a quiet way: the God who once carried you is the same God who strengthens you while you wait.

Carrying this into your week

Start with what God has done. When you feel the pull to earn your place, read Exodus 19:4 slowly and notice every verb God does.

Let obedience be a response. Ask, "Because I am His, how do I want to live today?" rather than "What do I have to do to be His?"

Remember you are near. God did not rescue you to keep you at a distance. He carried His people to Himself (Exodus 19:4), and in Christ He brings you near too.

For more passages on this, see Who God Says I Am. And if you are in a season of waiting, Jeremiah 29:11, read in context is a good companion.

Questions women often ask

Who does God say I am in Exodus 19?

God tells Israel He carried them out of Egypt and brought them to Himself, and calls them to be "my treasured possession among all peoples" and "a kingdom of priests and a holy nation" (Exodus 19:5-6). Peter applies this language to believers in Christ in 1 Peter 2:9.

What does "I bore you on eagles' wings" mean?

It is God's picture of how He rescued Israel from Egypt. He did the carrying, and the goal was nearness: "and brought you to myself" (Exodus 19:4).

Is Exodus 19:4 the same as Isaiah 40:31?

No. Exodus 19:4 describes God carrying His people out of slavery. Isaiah 40:31 promises that "they who wait for the LORD shall renew their strength; they shall mount up with wings like eagles." Both use the eagle image in different settings.
